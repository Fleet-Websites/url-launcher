### Fundamentals — the 12 principles

The 12 principles originated in Disney's internal practice (1930s–1940s) and were
published by Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston in *The Illusion of Life* (1981). They
remain the universal vocabulary across hand-drawn, digital, and stop-motion work.

| https://www.nyfa.edu/student-resources/12-principles-of-animation/ | `[S]` | NYFA — each principle explained with examples, free |
| https://academyofanimatedart.com/the-12-principles-of-animation-according-to-disney/ | `[S]` | Academy of Animated Art — the same twelve with visual breakdowns |

| https://academicworks.cuny.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1359&context=cc_oers | `[S]` | CUNY OER — 2D Animation Principles, open educational resource, freely downloadable |
| https://darkskiesfilm.com/how-to-learn-2d-and-3d-animation/ | `[S]` | Dark Skies Film — comprehensive guide to learning 2D and 3D animation from scratch |

**The one fact worth anchoring here:** squash-and-stretch is listed first in every
source not because it is the most important principle but because it is the easiest
observable test of whether an animation reads as alive. A ball that does not squash
on impact looks wrong before the viewer can say why. Start there.
